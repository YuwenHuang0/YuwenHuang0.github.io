When the Reward Is Wrong

Reward hacking in post-training, found step by step

After pre-training, chat models are post-trained on people's feedback. Today: why that reward can keep rising while people like the answers less, and where to look for the error.

3 · The surprise: the search exploits "longer is better"

Best-of-n keeps the answer r likes best, and r likes long answers. What happens as n grows?

Q: As n grows, r says the kept answer gets better and better. Predict what people say. A: Better at first, best near n = 16 to 32, then worse.

SoThe kept answers get longer and longer (length 1.97 at n = 1024): r rises, people's score falls. This is reward hacking.NextWatch the kept answer move as n grows.

3 · The surprise: the kept answer walks to long lengths

Each dot is one answer; best-of-n keeps the dot furthest along r's arrow.

Q: As n grows from 4 to 1024, where does the kept answer move? A: Ever longer, into the lengths people dislike.

SoThe more answers we search, the longer the kept one: exactly where r is wrong.NextWhy did no one notice? First, how we measured.

4 · Why no one noticed: too few ratings

We asked people to rate 20 kept answers for each n. Is that enough to see the fall?

SoNo: with 20 ratings the error bars (± 0.9) are larger than the fall (0.6); with 500 (± 0.2) the fall shows.NextSecond, what we asked.

4 · Why no one noticed: we asked r, not people

We also checked the kept answers with our reward model, then with a second one.

Q: Both reward models say the kept answers keep getting better. Should we believe them? A: No: both use the same formula, with the same blind spot. Ask people.

SoA reward model cannot check itself: ask people about the answers we keep.NextCollect the lessons.

Our error passed through five places; each can be checked and repaired.

Same 5000 picks; the new r = a · q + b · ℓ + c · ℓ² can take points off for "too long".

Conclusion

Trouble Searching harder raised the reward but made the answers worse: reward hacking.

Why The reward model's rule could not say "too long"; the search found that gap; few ratings and reward-model checks hid it.

Fix Give the reward model the missing term, keep the search near typical answers, and check with people.

Remember: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure (Goodhart's law).
