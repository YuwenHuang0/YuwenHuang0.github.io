\title{Optimal Maximum-Likelihood Tie-Breaking and a Reliability--Sensitivity Identity for Decoded Quantum Interferometry}

\author{Yuwen Huang and Wai Ho Mow%
\thanks{Manuscript draft, August 2026. Author affiliations and funding acknowledgments will be inserted before submission.}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A deterministic syndrome decoder used coherently in decoded quantum
interferometry (DQI) determines both its accepted probability and which
accepted errors interfere through one-bit pairs.  We optimize the latter
quantity while holding classical maximum-likelihood (ML) performance fixed.
The model is the phase-averaged separable binary DQI accepted-pair functional,
with independent probabilities $0<p_i<1/2$, positive coordinate rewards, and
deterministic exact-ML syndrome sections.  We solve this complete tie class.
The optimal rule minimizes weighted log-likelihood, maximizes an explicit
reward-weighted downward-edge potential within every ML tie, and resolves the
remaining tie by a degree-compatible monomial order.  Its representatives are
downward closed, and an exact two-term defect quantifies every competing ML
section.

The same optimizer yields a reliability--sensitivity identity: its optimal
interference mass equals an ideal product-state term plus a directional
derivative of the classical ML success probability.  In the identical-bit,
uniform-reward case, integration gives a finite-blocklength area law that
makes the complete optimal DQI-deficit curve and the ML reliability curve
determine one another.  At every syndrome rate $r$, there exist full-rank
linear syndrome-map sequences whose optimal exact-ML score has the explicit
Shannon-threshold limit
$1/2+\min\{p,q_r\}\sqrt{(1-p)/p}$ for the optimal score, where
$h_2(q_r)=r$.  The canonical section is also coefficientwise optimal
for every nonnegative radial amplitude profile.  Finally, a sharpened
ambiguity-mass bound identifies a no-gain regime.  Complementary constructions
give a positive-rate separation with polynomial-time exact ML and a connected,
coordinate-indecomposable linear-time witness without weight-two codewords.
Independent exhaustive
certificates verify the identities, constants, and stated scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{M}{aximum-likelihood} decoding treats each received syndrome
separately.  If several representatives have the same largest likelihood,
any deterministic tie rule gives the same frame-success probability.  This
observation makes tie-breaking largely invisible to conventional block-error
analysis; indeed, decoder ties do not change the error exponent of a code
sequence on the binary symmetric channel (BSC)~\cite{chang2021ties,chang2023ties}.

Decoded quantum interferometry (DQI) changes the performance functional.
DQI uses a reversible classical decoder inside a quantum circuit to reduce a
structured optimization problem to decoding~\cite{jordan2025dqi}.  After
postselection, the output quality depends both on the probability mass
accepted by the decoder and on coherent pairs of accepted errors.  Two
decoders can therefore have the same likelihood in every syndrome and still
prepare different states.  The distinction is operational: changing a tie at
one syndrome can destroy a one-bit pair with a neighboring syndrome even
though neither decoder incurs an additional classical frame error.

This paper asks a narrow coding-theoretic question.

\begin{quote}
\emph{Among deterministic ML syndrome decoders with identical conventional
performance, which tie rule maximizes the DQI accepted-pair objective?}
\end{quote}

The question differs from designing a decoder for bit-error probability or
another classical loss~\cite{kiely1995}.  It also differs from improving the
shell-wise imperfect-decoding bounds in the original DQI analysis and recent
extensions~\cite{jordan2025dqi,gao2026}.  Shell counts describe how much
probability mass a decoder accepts at each Hamming weight.  The exact DQI term
also records how those accepted errors are joined by edges of the Hamming
cube.  The latter is a two-point statistic.
The main result below nevertheless shows that its unrestricted ML optimum has
a universal canonical form.

\subsection{Contributions and scope}

Within the phase-averaged separable binary accepted-pair model, the paper
makes four principal contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item We solve the complete class of deterministic exact-ML syndrome sections
for independent binary product priors $0<p_i<1/2$ and positive coordinate
rewards.  The optimal syndrome-local rule minimizes weighted log-likelihood,
maximizes an explicit reward-weighted downward-edge potential, and then uses a
fixed degree-compatible multiplicative monomial order.  Its image is downward
closed.  An exact gap identity separates fiberwise potential loss from missing
boundary edges and characterizes every optimizer.

\item We connect the optimum exactly to classical decoding reliability.  The
optimal accepted-edge mass is an ideal product-state term plus a one-sided
directional derivative of $Z_{\rm ML}(\boldsymbol p)$.  In the radial case this
becomes a differential identity and a finite-blocklength area law for
$-m^{-1}\log Z_{\rm ML}(p)$.  Product-measure derivative formulas are
classical~\cite{margulis1974,russo1981}; the new point is that DQI-optimal ML
  tie-breaking realizes the active directional derivative and converts it into
  an exact interference score.  As a concrete coding consequence, we prove the
  existence of full-rank linear syndrome-map sequences at every fixed syndrome rate and
  derive for those sequences a sharp Shannon threshold, correct-decoding exponent, limiting
optimal DQI score, and integrated DQI deficit.

\item In the identical-bit, uniform-reward setting, one canonical section is
coefficientwise optimal in every Hamming layer and therefore maximizes every
nonnegative radial accepted-edge mass simultaneously.  The monotone structure
of suitably ordered coset leaders is classical
\cite{helleseth2005,yasunaga2010,borges2010coset,borges2012leaders}; our result
is its DQI coefficientwise optimality, closed-form optimum, and exact defect.

\item We prove a stability bound in terms of the total selected ML mass on
ambiguous syndromes.  If this mass vanishes relative to acceptance, all
deterministic ML tie rules have asymptotically identical DQI scores.  A
  quotient lift and a coordinate-indecomposable fixed-distance family show that
  a nonvanishing separation is possible when an ambiguity budget remains.  A
  logarithmic-block construction further gives positive rate, nonvanishing
  acceptance, and polynomial-time exact ML.  In all cases the compared rules
  have identical ML likelihood, acceptance, Hamming-layer profile, and matched
  asymptotic decoding cost.
\end{enumerate}

The accepted-pair expression itself comes from the imperfect-decoder DQI
analysis~\cite{jordan2025dqi}; coordinate-weighted versions appear in
multivariate DQI~\cite{bu2026}.  Weighted-Hamming ML decoding, canonical
coset-leader down-sets, Maximum Section, and DE-NFG theory are also prior
tools~\cite{bitzer2024,grochow2018,cao2017}.  We use them without claiming
their invention.  Secondary sections record a paired estimator, a restricted
compatible-section formulation, and a real-PSD DE-NFG realization for offline
evaluation.

The results do not establish quantum supremacy, a universal gain over all ML
decoders, or an asymptotic gain for generic LDPC ensembles.  Such claims depend
on the optimization instance and on the full reversible cost.  Recent work
likewise emphasizes that DQI advantage is structure dependent
\cite{anschuetz2025,kramer2026}.

\section{Decoder Sections and the DQI Pair Functional}
\label{sec:metric}

Unless a base is displayed explicitly, $\log$ denotes the natural logarithm.

Let $H\in\F_2^{\rho\times m}$ have full row rank, let
$C=\ker H$, and let $\mathcal S=\im H=\F_2^\rho$.  A deterministic syndrome
decoder is a section
\begin{equation}
 D:\mathcal S\longrightarrow\F_2^m,\qquad HD(s)=s.
 \label{eq:section}
\end{equation}
Its image $T_D=D(\mathcal S)$ contains one representative from every coset of
$C$.  We interchangeably call $D$ a decoder and $T_D$ a transversal.

Write $\mathbf e_i$ for the $i$th unit vector and $e_i$ for coordinate $i$ of
$e$.  Let $\{a_e:e\in\F_2^m\}$ be nonnegative real amplitudes normalized by
$\sum_ea_e^2=1$.  Let coordinate $i$ have reward $\gamma_i>0$ and put
$\Gamma=\sum_i\gamma_i$.  Define the accepted norm and weighted undirected
accepted-edge mass
\begin{align}
 Z_{D,a}&=\sum_{e\in T_D}a_e^2,
 \label{eq:radial-Z}\\
 \mathcal E_{D,a,\gamma}
 &=\sum_{e\in T_D}\sum_{\substack{i:e_i=0\\e+\mathbf e_i\in T_D}}
 \gamma_i a_ea_{e+\mathbf e_i}.
 \label{eq:radial-E}
\end{align}
Every Hamming-cube edge is counted once.

\begin{theorem}[Generalized accepted-pair identity]
\label{thm:section-identity}
For the phase-averaged binary DQI objective,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal J_{D,a,\gamma}
 &=\frac12+\frac{\mathcal E_{D,a,\gamma}}{\Gamma Z_{D,a}},
 \label{eq:radial-J}\\
 R_{D,a,\gamma}&:=Z_{D,a}
 \left(\mathcal J_{D,a,\gamma}-\frac12\right)
          =\frac{\mathcal E_{D,a,\gamma}}\Gamma.
 \label{eq:radial-R}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Jordan \emph{et al.} derive the deterministic imperfect-decoder pair
expression for shell amplitudes, and Bu \emph{et al.} give its weighted
block-objective extension~\cite[Supplementary Secs.~10.3--10.4]{jordan2025dqi}%
~\cite{bu2026}.  For completeness, the phase-averaged functional is the
expectation of the following normalized accepted-state observable:
\begin{equation}
 |\phi_{D,a}\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt{Z_{D,a}}}
    \sum_{e\in T_D}a_e|e\rangle,\qquad
 O_\gamma=\frac12I+\frac1{2\Gamma}\sum_i\gamma_iX_i.
 \label{eq:accepted-state-observable}
\end{equation}
Expanding $\langle\phi_{D,a}|O_\gamma|\phi_{D,a}\rangle$ counts both
orientations of every accepted $i$-edge through $X_i$; the factor $1/2$
leaves each undirected edge once.  This proves~\eqref{eq:radial-J}, and
multiplication by $Z_{D,a}$ proves~\eqref{eq:radial-R}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a direct arbitrary-amplitude, separable-reward extension of
the cited DQI identities; the results below optimize the decoder within this
functional.  The rewards $\gamma_i$ cover the separable coordinate-weighted
binary case motivated by multivariate DQI~\cite{bu2026}; they do not cover its
complete polynomial family.  A radial profile has
$a_e=a_{\wt(e)}$ and normalization
$\sum_k\binom{m}{k}a_k^2=1$.  For the Bernoulli radial profile, fix
$0<p<1/2$, set $\gamma_i=1$, and write
\begin{equation}
 a_k=p^{k/2}(1-p)^{(m-k)/2},\qquad q=\frac{p}{1-p}.
 \label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
The product-amplitude specialization is operationally elementary: for
independent probabilities $p_i$, the input state is
\begin{equation}
 |\psi_{\boldsymbol p}\rangle
 =\bigotimes_{i=1}^m
 \left(\sqrt{1-p_i}\,|0\rangle+\sqrt{p_i}\,|1\rangle\right).
 \label{eq:product-state}
\end{equation}
It is prepared by one single-qubit rotation per coordinate, followed by the
same coherent evaluation and uncomputation of the deterministic decoder used
in DQI.  Thus the product-prior results optimize an efficiently preparable
separable specialization of the phase-averaged binary functional rather than
an abstract amplitude oracle.  We do not assert that every finite-degree
shell-polynomial state in the original DQI construction has this product
form.
Define
\begin{align}
 W_D(q)&=\sum_{e\in T_D}q^{\wt(e)},
 \label{eq:W}\\
 P_D(q)&=\sum_{\substack{\{e,e'\}\subseteq T_D\\d_H(e,e')=1}}
 q^{\min\{\wt(e),\wt(e')\}}.
 \label{eq:P}
\end{align}
Then Theorem~\ref{thm:section-identity} specializes to
\begin{align}
 Z_D&=(1-p)^mW_D(q),
 \label{eq:Z}\\
 \mathcal J_D&=\frac12+\frac{\sqrt q}{m}\frac{P_D(q)}{W_D(q)},
 \label{eq:J}\\
 R_D&=(1-p)^m\frac{\sqrt q}{m}P_D(q).
 \label{eq:R}
\end{align}

The decomposition is central.  Conventional ML decoding maximizes one vertex
weight independently in every coset.  Equation~\eqref{eq:P} couples adjacent
cosets.  Consequently, equal $W_D$ does not imply equal $P_D$.

\subsection{A paired-sampling identity}

The pair term can be estimated without enumerating the transversal.  Draw a
coordinate $I$ uniformly from $\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and draw the other $m-1$ bits
i.i.d. from $\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$.  Let $E^{(0)}$ and $E^{(1)}$ be the
two errors obtained by fixing bit $I$ to zero and one.  Define
$A_D(e)=\ind\{D(He)=e\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Unbiased pair estimator]
\label{prop:paired-sampling}
Put
\begin{equation}
 \Xi_D:=\Pr\{A_D(E^{(0)})=1,\ A_D(E^{(1)})=1\}.
 \label{eq:paired-event}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 R_D=\frac{(1-p)^m\sqrt q}{m}P_D(q)
     =\sqrt{p(1-p)}\,\Xi_D.
 \label{eq:paired-sampling}
\end{equation}
Thus two deterministic decoder calls give a bounded unbiased sample of
$R_D$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed coordinate and fixed values of the other bits, the sampling
probability of the common $m-1$-bit pattern is exactly the product prior with
the varying bit removed.  Multiplication by $\sqrt{p(1-p)}$ restores the
geometric mean of the two endpoint probabilities.  Averaging over $I$ counts
every accepted undirected edge once and divides by $m$.
\end{proof}

Hoeffding's inequality gives additive accuracy $\varepsilon$ with
$O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta))$ paired samples at confidence $1-\delta$,
independent of blocklength.  Estimating the conditional score additionally
requires stable division by $Z_D$; this is well conditioned when $Z_D$ is
bounded below.  Equation~\eqref{eq:paired-sampling} is the matched baseline
for any approximate DE-NFG or Bethe estimator.

\section{Universal Optima over ML Tie Rules}
\label{sec:universal-ml}

\subsection{Asymmetric product priors}

First consider independent, nonidentical error bits with
$0<p_i<1/2$ and
\begin{equation}
 \pi(e)=\prod_{i=1}^m p_i^{e_i}(1-p_i)^{1-e_i}.
 \label{eq:product-prior}
\end{equation}
Use the product amplitudes $a_e=\sqrt{\pi(e)}$ and retain arbitrary positive
coordinate rewards $\gamma_i$ from Section~\ref{sec:metric}.
In this subsection, write
$\mathcal E_{\gamma,T}:=\mathcal E_{T,\sqrt{\pi},\gamma}$ and
$Z_T:=Z_{T,\sqrt{\pi}}$.
Define
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_i=\log\frac{1-p_i}{p_i},\qquad
 r_i=e^{\lambda_i/2}=\sqrt{\frac{1-p_i}{p_i}},
 \label{eq:lambda-r}
\end{equation}
and, for a binary word $e$,
\begin{equation}
 L(e)=\sum_i\lambda_i e_i,\qquad
 G_\gamma(e)=\sum_{i:e_i=1}\gamma_i r_i.
 \label{eq:L-G}
\end{equation}
Exact ML decoding minimizes $L(e)$ within each syndrome class.  The second
quantity is the total reward-weighted amplitude of all downward incidences
charged to $e$.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal ML tie rule for product priors and coordinate rewards]
\label{thm:asymmetric-optimum}
For every product prior~\eqref{eq:product-prior} and every $\gamma_i>0$, apply
the ordered rule
\begin{align}
 &\text{primary: minimize }L(e),\nonumber\\
 &\text{secondary: maximize }G_\gamma(e),\nonumber\\
 &\text{tertiary: take the least word in a fixed degree-compatible}
 \nonumber\\[-0.2em]
 &\hspace{5.7em}\text{multiplicative monomial order}.
 \label{eq:product-rule}
\end{align}
Its image $T_\star$ is downward closed and globally maximizes the DQI
accepted-edge mass over all deterministic ML sections.

More precisely, let $e_s$ be the representative chosen by any ML section
$T$, let $e_s^\star$ be the choice in~\eqref{eq:product-rule}, and let
$\pi_s=\pi(e_s)=\pi(e_s^\star)$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 B_\downarrow(T)=\sum_s\pi_s\sum_{i:e_{s,i}=1}\gamma_i r_i
 \ind\{e_s+\mathbf e_i\notin T\}.
 \label{eq:asym-boundary}
\end{equation}
Then the exact accepted-edge mass and exact optimality gap are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal E_{\gamma,T}
 &=\sum_s\pi_sG_\gamma(e_s)-B_\downarrow(T),
 \label{eq:asym-edge}\\
 \mathcal E_{\gamma,T_\star}-\mathcal E_{\gamma,T}
 &=\sum_s\pi_s\,[G_\gamma(e_s^\star)-G_\gamma(e_s)]
   +B_\downarrow(T).
 \label{eq:asym-defect}
\end{align}
 Both terms in~\eqref{eq:asym-defect} are nonnegative.  Consequently, an ML
 section is optimal if and only if it maximizes $G_\gamma$ in every ML fiber
 and its image is downward closed.  All ML sections have
the same acceptance $Z_{\rm ML}=\sum_s\pi_s$, so $T_\star$ also maximizes the
conditional score $1/2+\mathcal E_{\gamma,T}/(\Gamma Z_{\rm ML})$ and its
acceptance-weighted per-attempt excess.  Scaling all $\gamma_i$ by a common
constant does not change the optimizer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $e_i=1$, then
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_i\sqrt{\pi(e)\pi(e+\mathbf e_i)}=\pi(e)\gamma_i r_i.
 \label{eq:asym-edge-charge}
\end{equation}
Charging every accepted edge to its endpoint containing the one in its
varying coordinate proves~\eqref{eq:asym-edge}.  The rule in
\eqref{eq:product-rule} maximizes the first term independently in every ML
fiber.

It remains to prove downward closure.  Let $e$ be selected with $e_i=1$ and
put $f=e+\mathbf e_i$.  The word $f$ is ML in its coset.  Indeed, if a coset mate
$v$ had $L(v)<L(f)$, then $v+\mathbf e_i$ would lie in the coset of $e$ and have
cost at most $L(v)+\lambda_i<L(e)$.  Suppose that the rule selects
$f'\ne f$ in the coset of
$f$.  Both have the same cost.  Coordinate $i$ cannot belong to
$\operatorname{supp}(f')$, because then
$L(f'+\mathbf e_i)=L(e)-2\lambda_i$.  Hence $f'+\mathbf e_i$ is an ML candidate in the coset
of $e$, and
\begin{equation}
 G_\gamma(f'+\mathbf e_i)-G_\gamma(e)=G_\gamma(f')-G_\gamma(f).
 \label{eq:asym-G-transfer}
\end{equation}
If $G_\gamma(f')>G_\gamma(f)$, this contradicts the secondary choice of $e$.
If the two values agree, adjoining the same absent coordinate preserves the
multiplicative monomial order and contradicts the tertiary choice.  Thus $f$ is
selected.  Downward closure gives $B_\downarrow(T_\star)=0$; subtracting
\eqref{eq:asym-edge} for $T$ from that for $T_\star$ proves
\eqref{eq:asym-defect}.
\end{proof}

The rule remains syndrome-local.  An exact weighted-ML list decoder first
retains all minimum-$L$ candidates, then takes the largest $G_\gamma$, and finally
uses the fixed monomial-order key.  A trellis or junction-tree solver can
carry $(L,-G_\gamma,\text{key})$ as its ordered path metric.  This resolves tie
design; it does not make exact ML decoding polynomial for arbitrary codes.
The asymmetric theorem is most relevant when reliabilities repeat or are
grouped or quantized; with generic incommensurate likelihoods, most fibers have
no ML tie and the secondary rule is vacuous.

The same rule has a second interpretation that links the DQI optimum directly
to a standard classical reliability function.  Define the optimal ML success
probability
\begin{equation}
 Z_{\rm ML}(\boldsymbol p)
 :=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\max_{e:He=s}\pi_{\boldsymbol p}(e).
 \label{eq:asym-ZML}
\end{equation}
For a direction $\boldsymbol v$, let
$D_{\boldsymbol v}^{+}$ denote the right directional derivative.

\begin{theorem}[Reliability-sensitivity identity]
\label{thm:reliability-sensitivity}
Fix $0<p_i<1/2$ and $\gamma_i>0$, and define
\begin{align}
 v_i&=\gamma_i\sqrt{p_i}(1-p_i)^{3/2},
 \label{eq:sensitivity-direction}\\
 b_\gamma(\boldsymbol p)
 &=\sum_{i=1}^m\gamma_i\sqrt{p_i(1-p_i)}.
 \label{eq:ideal-bias}
\end{align}
Then the right directional derivative of~\eqref{eq:asym-ZML} exists and
\begin{align}
 \max_{T\ {\rm ML}}\mathcal E_{\gamma,T}
 &=D_{\boldsymbol v}^{+}Z_{\rm ML}(\boldsymbol p)
   +b_\gamma(\boldsymbol p)Z_{\rm ML}(\boldsymbol p),
 \label{eq:sensitivity-edge}\\
 \max_{T\ {\rm ML}}\mathcal J_{\gamma,T}
 &=\frac12+\frac{b_\gamma(\boldsymbol p)}{\Gamma}
   +\frac1\Gamma D_{\boldsymbol v}^{+}
       \log Z_{\rm ML}(\boldsymbol p).
 \label{eq:sensitivity-score}
\end{align}
The directional derivative is nonpositive.  Hence the ideal product-state
bias $b_\gamma/\Gamma$ is an upper bound, and its exact loss is the
directional sensitivity of the classical ML success probability.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Along $\boldsymbol p+t\boldsymbol v$,
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\log\pi_{\boldsymbol p+t\boldsymbol v}(e)
 \right|_{t=0}
 =G_\gamma(e)-b_\gamma(\boldsymbol p).
 \label{eq:score-derivative}
\end{equation}
The maximum in each syndrome fiber is over a finite set.  The right
derivative of that maximum is therefore the largest derivative among its
active ML candidates.  The secondary rule in
Theorem~\ref{thm:asymmetric-optimum} selects exactly that candidate.  Summing
\eqref{eq:score-derivative} over syndrome fibers gives
\eqref{eq:sensitivity-edge}; division by the common acceptance and by
$\Gamma$ gives~\eqref{eq:sensitivity-score}.

The selected set is downward closed.  The probability of any fixed downward-
closed subset of the Boolean cube is nonincreasing when any $p_i$ increases,
by the standard monotone coupling of product Bernoulli measures.  The active
section selected above realizes the right derivative of $Z_{\rm ML}$, so
$D_{\boldsymbol v}^{+}Z_{\rm ML}\le0$.
\end{proof}

For the identical-bit Bernoulli profile, the sensitivity identity has an
exact integral form.  Let $\mathcal J_{\rm ML}^{\star}(p)$ denote the optimum
over deterministic ML sections and define its deficit from the full-cube
bias by
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\rm DQI}(p)
 :=\frac12+\sqrt{p(1-p)}-\mathcal J_{\rm ML}^{\star}(p)\ge0.
 \label{eq:dqi-deficit}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Radial derivative and area identities]
\label{cor:radial-sensitivity}
For $0<p<1/2$,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal J_{\rm ML}^{\star}(p)
 &=\frac12+\sqrt{p(1-p)}
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad
 +\frac{\sqrt p(1-p)^{3/2}}{m}
   \frac{d}{dp}\log Z_{\rm ML}(p),
 \label{eq:radial-sensitivity}\\
 -\frac1m\log Z_{\rm ML}(p)
 &=\int_0^p
 \frac{\mathcal L_{\rm DQI}(u)}
 {\sqrt u(1-u)^{3/2}}\,du.
 \label{eq:radial-area}
\end{align}
Thus the complete optimal DQI deficit curve and the classical ML reliability
curve determine one another.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
When all $p_i=p$ and all $\gamma_i=1$, the direction in
\eqref{eq:sensitivity-direction} is constant across coordinates.  Hence
 $D_{\boldsymbol v}^{+}\log Z_{\rm ML}
=\sqrt p(1-p)^{3/2}(d/dp)\log Z_{\rm ML}$, while
$b_\gamma=m\sqrt{p(1-p)}$ and $\Gamma=m$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:sensitivity-score} therefore gives
\eqref{eq:radial-sensitivity}.  Rearranging and integrating from zero, where
$Z_{\rm ML}(0)=1$, proves~\eqref{eq:radial-area}.
\end{proof}

This identity is distinct from the EXIT/GEXIT area theorem for channel
decoding~\cite{measson2009}.  EXIT/GEXIT integrates an extrinsic-information
quantity and connects iterative and a posteriori decoding thresholds.  Here
the integrand is the phase-averaged DQI accepted-pair deficit, and the identity
holds because the DQI-optimal ML tie rule realizes the active derivative of
the exact correct-decoding probability.  No belief-propagation approximation
or extrinsic-information interpretation enters the statement.

Define the normalized classical ML reliability exponent
\begin{equation}
 I_m(p):=-\frac1m\log Z_{{\rm ML},m}(p).
 \label{eq:normalized-reliability-exponent}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Asymptotic area law]
\label{cor:asymptotic-area}
For every code sequence and $0\le a<b<1/2$,
\begin{equation}
 \int_a^b\frac{\mathcal L_{{\rm DQI},m}(u)}
 {\sqrt u(1-u)^{3/2}}\,du=I_m(b)-I_m(a).
 \label{eq:interval-area}
\end{equation}
Hence convergence of $I_m$ at the two endpoints determines the limiting
weighted area on $[a,b]$ without any exchange of derivatives and limits.  In
particular, subexponential ML success loss at $p$,
$Z_{{\rm ML},m}(p)=\exp[-o(m)]$, forces the weighted area on $[0,p]$ to vanish;
an exponent $Z_{{\rm ML},m}(p)=\exp[-mI(p)+o(m)]$ gives limiting area $I(p)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Subtract~\eqref{eq:radial-area} at $a$ and $b$.  The two asymptotic statements
then follow directly and require no pointwise convergence of the deficit.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A Shannon-threshold consequence for linear syndrome maps}

The area law becomes explicit for fixed-rate linear source coding.  Write
\begin{align}
 h_2(x)&=-x\log_2x-(1-x)\log_2(1-x),\\
 D_{\rm b}(x\Vert p)&=x\log\frac{x}{p}
 +(1-x)\log\frac{1-x}{1-p}.
 \label{eq:binary-entropy-divergence}
\end{align}
For a syndrome rate $r=\rho/m\in(0,1)$, equivalently a channel-code rate
$1-r$, let $q_r\in(0,1/2)$ be the unique solution
of $h_2(q_r)=r$.  The exponent below is the Bernoulli specialization of the
fixed-rate correct-decoding exponent; attainability by linear syndrome codes
is classical~\cite{chen2009duality}.  We include a direct argument because the
resulting DQI score law is new.

\begin{theorem}[Linear-syndrome Shannon threshold]
\label{thm:linear-shannon-dqi}
For every $r\in(0,1)$, with $\rho_m=\lfloor rm\rfloor$, there exists a
sequence of full-row-rank binary maps
$H_m\in\F_2^{\rho_m\times m}$ such that, simultaneously for
every fixed $0<p<1/2$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{m\to\infty} I_m(p)=I_r(p):=
 \begin{cases}
  0, & p\le q_r,\\
  D_{\rm b}(q_r\Vert p), & p>q_r.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:linear-source-exponent}
\end{equation}
For the canonical DQI-optimal deterministic exact-ML section with uniform
coordinate rewards,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\mathcal J_{{\rm ML},m}^{\star}(p)
 =\frac12+\min\{p,q_r\}\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p}}.
 \label{eq:linear-source-dqi-score}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{m\to\infty}
 \int_0^p\frac{\mathcal L_{{\rm DQI},m}(u)}
 {\sqrt u(1-u)^{3/2}}\,du=I_r(p).
 \label{eq:linear-source-dqi-area}
\end{equation}
Thus the classical source-coding threshold $r=h_2(p)$ is also the point at
which the limiting optimal DQI score leaves its full-cube value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every syndrome transversal contains exactly $2^{\rho_m}$ words.  Since
$p<1/2$, Bernoulli word probabilities decrease with Hamming weight.  Hence
the success mass of any transversal is at most the mass of the
$2^{\rho_m}$ most probable words.  Hamming-ball asymptotics and the method of
types give the universal lower bound
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}I_m(p)\ge I_r(p).
 \label{eq:linear-source-converse}
\end{equation}

For achievability, draw a $\rho_m\times m$ binary matrix with independent
uniform entries, where $\rho_m=\lfloor rm\rfloor$.  Put
$\epsilon_m=m^{-1/3}$ and
$a_m=\lfloor m(q_r-\epsilon_m)\rfloor$.  For an error $e$ of weight $w$, a
union bound gives
\begin{align}
 &\Pr_H\{\exists e'\ne e:\ \wt(e')\le w,\ He'=He\}
 \nonumber\\
 &\hspace{28mm}\le 2^{-\rho_m}\sum_{j=0}^{w}\binom mj.
 \label{eq:random-syndrome-collision}
\end{align}
Uniformly for $w\le a_m$, the right side is at most
$\exp[-c m^{2/3}]$ for some $c=c(r)>0$ and all sufficiently large $m$.
Markov's inequality at level $\exp[-c m^{2/3}/2]$, followed by a union bound
over the $m+1$ shells, shows that, with probability tending to one, the
fraction of colliding words in every shell $w\le a_m$ is $o(1)$.  A random
rectangular binary matrix is also full row rank with probability tending to
one.  Select one matrix satisfying both properties.  Every noncolliding word
is the unique ML representative of
its syndrome, so its ML success probability obeys
\begin{equation}
 Z_{{\rm ML},m}(p)\ge(1-o(1))
 \Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(m,p)\le a_m\}.
 \label{eq:random-syndrome-lower}
\end{equation}
For $p<q_r$ the right side tends to one.  For $p=q_r$ it is
$\exp[-o(m)]$, and for $p>q_r$ its exponent tends to
$D_{\rm b}(q_r\Vert p)$.  Together with
\eqref{eq:linear-source-converse}, this proves
\eqref{eq:linear-source-exponent}.  The shell property is independent of
$p$, so the selected sequence works simultaneously for every fixed $p$.
At the boundary $p=q_r$, the conclusion is only a zero normalized exponent;
it does not assert that $Z_{{\rm ML},m}(q_r)$ tends to one.

Let $T_m$ be the canonical downward-closed ML section and define its
accepted conditional relative weight
\begin{equation}
 \bar w_m(p):=\frac{1}{mZ_{{\rm ML},m}(p)}
 \sum_{e\in T_m}\pi_p(e)\wt(e).
 \label{eq:accepted-relative-weight}
\end{equation}
If $p<q_r$, \eqref{eq:random-syndrome-lower} gives $Z_{{\rm ML},m}(p)\to1$;
because $0\le \wt(e)/m\le1$, conditioning on acceptance then gives
$\bar w_m(p)\to p$.  Let $p\ge q_r$ and fix a sufficiently small
$\delta>0$.  The accepted mass below $(q_r-\delta)m$ is bounded by the
corresponding Bernoulli lower tail, whose exponent is strictly larger than
$I_r(p)$.  The accepted mass above $(q_r+\delta)m$ is at most
$2^{\rho_m}$ times the largest probability of a word in that range; its
exponent is also strictly larger than $I_r(p)$.  Dividing these two bounds by
$Z_{{\rm ML},m}(p)=\exp[-mI_r(p)+o(m)]$ shows that both conditional tails
vanish.  Since $\delta>0$ is arbitrary, $\bar w_m(p)\to q_r$.

Every one-bit edge of a downward-closed section can be charged to its upper
endpoint.  Therefore the exact optimal score is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal J_{{\rm ML},m}^{\star}(p)
 =\frac12+\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p}}\,\bar w_m(p),
 \label{eq:score-from-accepted-weight}
\end{equation}
which proves~\eqref{eq:linear-source-dqi-score}.  Finally,
\eqref{eq:linear-source-dqi-area} follows from
Corollary~\ref{cor:asymptotic-area} and
\eqref{eq:linear-source-exponent}.
\end{proof}

The limiting score in~\eqref{eq:linear-source-dqi-score} is continuous and
strictly increases up to $p=q_r$, then strictly decreases.  It therefore has
a unique cusp maximum $1/2+\sqrt{q_r(1-q_r)}$ at the Shannon threshold.  Above
threshold, the limiting deficit is explicitly
$(p-q_r)\sqrt{(1-p)/p}$, whose area-law integrand is
$\frac{d}{dp}D_{\rm b}(q_r\Vert p)$.

\subsection{A coefficientwise radial companion}

Set $\gamma_i=1$, suppress $\gamma$ from the notation, and let the amplitudes
depend only on Hamming weight.
For each syndrome $s$, let
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_s=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{e:He=s}\wt(e),
 \qquad \ell_s=\wt(e)\quad(e\in\mathcal L_s),
 \label{eq:ml-list}
\end{equation}
and let $n_k=|\{s:\ell_s=k\}|$.  Every deterministic ML section selects
exactly $n_k$ words in layer $k$.  For an ML transversal $T$, let $N_k(T)$
be the number of accepted Hamming edges between layers $k-1$ and $k$.

\begin{theorem}[Coefficientwise optimal ML section]
\label{thm:universal-ml}
For every binary linear code and every deterministic ML section $T$,
\begin{equation}
 N_k(T)\le k n_k,\qquad 1\le k\le m.
 \label{eq:layer-bound}
\end{equation}
Fix a coordinate order and, within every coset, choose a minimum-weight word;
among ties, choose the lexicographically first word with $0<1$.  The resulting
section $T_{\rm glex}$ attains equality simultaneously for every $k$.
Consequently, $T_{\rm glex}$ maximizes $\mathcal E_{T,a}$ and $R_{T,a}$ for
every nonnegative radial amplitude profile $a$.  It also maximizes the
conditional score $\mathcal J_{T,a}$ whenever the common ML acceptance
$Z_{{\rm ML},a}$ is positive.

More precisely, define the layer defect
\begin{equation}
 \delta_k(T):=k n_k-N_k(T).
 \label{eq:layer-defect}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
 Z_{{\rm ML},a}&=\sum_{k=0}^m n_k a_k^2,
 \label{eq:general-ml-Z}\\
 \mathcal E_{T,a}
 &=\sum_{k=1}^m a_{k-1}a_k\,[k n_k-\delta_k(T)].
 \label{eq:general-defect}
\end{align}
The optimum has every $\delta_k=0$.  If $a_{k-1}a_k>0$ for all occupied
layers, an ML section is optimal exactly when its selected set is downward
closed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every selected word $e$ of weight $k$ has exactly $k$ downward neighbors
$e+\mathbf e_i$, one for each coordinate $i$ with $e_i=1$.  Charge each
accepted edge to its upper endpoint.
This gives~\eqref{eq:layer-bound}, and the missing downward incidences are
exactly $\delta_k(T)$.  Every edge between layers $k-1$ and $k$ contributes
$a_{k-1}a_k$, which proves~\eqref{eq:general-defect}.

It remains to prove the claimed closure.  Graded lexicographic order is a
degree-compatible multiplicative monomial order $\prec$ on squarefree
monomials.  More generally, choose the $\prec$-least binary word in every code
coset under any such order.
Degree compatibility makes each choice an ML representative.  We show that
the selected set is downward closed.  Let $e$ be selected, let $e_i=1$, and
 put $f=e+\mathbf e_i$.  The word $f$ is a coset leader: a lower-weight word in its
coset would, after toggling coordinate $i$, give a word lighter than $e$ in
the coset of $e$.  If $f$ were not selected, the selected word $f'$ in its
coset would have the same weight and satisfy $f'\prec f$.  Coordinate $i$
cannot lie in the support of $f'$, since $f'+\mathbf e_i$ would then be lighter than
$e$ in the coset of $e$.  Multiplicativity therefore gives
$f'+\mathbf e_i\prec f+\mathbf e_i=e$, again contradicting the choice of $e$.  Hence every
downward neighbor of every selected word is selected.
\end{proof}

The rule is syndrome-local, yet its complete image is globally downward
closed.  The monotone structure of correctable errors, weight-compatible
orders, and Gr\"obner representations of binary codes are classical
\cite{helleseth2005,yasunaga2010,borges2010coset,borges2012leaders}.  The new
conclusion here is that
their downward-closed canonical section is coefficientwise optimal for the
DQI pair functional.  Constructing the complete coset-leader table can still
be exponential in the code codimension.  The theorem gives the exact global
target; compact realizations require additional code structure.

For the Bernoulli profile, put
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm ML}(q)=\sum_{k=0}^m n_kq^k.
 \label{eq:ml-weight-enumerator}
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-ml} yields the closed form
\begin{align}
 \max_{D\ {\rm ML}}P_D(q)&=W'_{\rm ML}(q),
 \label{eq:ml-P-opt}\\
 \max_{D\ {\rm ML}}\mathcal J_D
 &=\frac12+\frac{\sqrt q}{m}
   \frac{W'_{\rm ML}(q)}{W_{\rm ML}(q)}.
 \label{eq:ml-J-opt}
\end{align}
For any ML section $T$, the exact polynomial defect is
\begin{align}
 P_T(q)&=W'_{\rm ML}(q)-\Delta_\downarrow(T;q),
 \label{eq:down-defect}\\
 \Delta_\downarrow(T;q)
 &=\sum_{e\in T}\sum_{i:e_i=1}q^{\wt(e)-1}
   \ind\{e+\mathbf e_i\notin T\}.
 \label{eq:down-defect-def}
\end{align}
Thus the same canonical section is optimal for every $0<p<1/2$, without
retuning the tie rule.

\section{ML Candidate Compatibility and Restricted Tie Design}
\label{sec:general-ml}

Let $h_i=H\mathbf e_i$ denote column $i$ of $H$.  For each syndrome $s$, use the
complete minimum-weight list $\mathcal L_s$ from~\eqref{eq:ml-list}.
Every deterministic ML decoder is a selection $D(s)\in\mathcal L_s$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact compatible-section objective for ML ties]
\label{thm:general-maximum-section}
Every deterministic ML selection has the same denominator
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm ML}(q)=\sum_s q^{\ell_s},\qquad
 Z_{\rm ML}=(1-p)^mW_{\rm ML}(q).
 \label{eq:ml-denominator}
\end{equation}
Its accepted-edge polynomial is
\begin{equation}
 P(D;q)=\frac12\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\sum_{i=1}^m
 q^{\min\{\ell_s,\ell_{s+h_i}\}}
 \ind\{D(s+h_i)=D(s)+\mathbf e_i\}.
 \label{eq:general-max-section}
\end{equation}
Thus every ML tie rule has an exact finite weighted compatible-section
representation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All candidates in $\mathcal L_s$ have weight $\ell_s$, which proves
\eqref{eq:ml-denominator}.  Representatives at syndromes $s$ and $s+h_i$
form a physical one-bit pair in coordinate $i$ precisely when the indicator
in~\eqref{eq:general-max-section} equals one.  The factor $1/2$ removes the two
orientations of a syndrome edge.  Repeated columns of $H$ remain distinct
physical coordinates and are retained in the inner sum.  Since the
denominator is fixed, all three optimization criteria have the same maximizers.
\end{proof}

An equivalent graph construction makes the optimization precise.  Place the list
$\mathcal L_s$ above each syndrome vertex $s$.  Connect $e\in\mathcal L_s$ to
$e+\mathbf e_i\in\mathcal L_{s+h_i}$ with the weight in
\eqref{eq:general-max-section}.  A decoder selects one lifted vertex above
each base vertex, and its reward is the weight of compatible selected pairs.
For arbitrary ML lists, an edge may induce only a partial matching, so this is
a partial-permutation label problem rather than a covering graph in general.
When every edge induces a full bijection on a common label set, it is Maximum
Section of a covering graph.  The relation of that subclass to Unique Games
and Max-2Lin is known~\cite{grochow2018}.  The structure added here is the
code-induced quotient, the Bernoulli radial weights, and the DQI pair
functional.  Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-ml} already solves the unrestricted
ML optimization.  The candidate graph remains useful when an implementation
restricts the available labels or when a larger, possibly non-ML, candidate
set is optimized.

If every list contains two labels and every active edge gives a full matching,
label the candidates by $b_s\in\F_2$.  Compatibility then has the form
\begin{equation}
 b_t=b_s+\delta_{st}.
 \label{eq:max2lin}
\end{equation}
On a tree, labels propagate exactly.  On a loopy graph, zero frustration is
equivalent to the XOR of $\delta_{st}$ vanishing around every cycle.  For a
label alphabet of size $L$, exact max-sum on a tree costs
$O(\sum_{(s,t)}|\mathcal L_s||\mathcal L_t|)$, and a graph with $N$ vertices
and treewidth $\kappa$ admits exact junction-tree optimization in
$O(NL^{\kappa+1})$ time and $O(NL^\kappa)$ memory.  These are structural
algorithms: the explicit syndrome graph has $2^\rho$ vertices, so scalable
use requires a compact decoder architecture or an exploitable factorization.

\section{A Quotient Lift with an Exact Weighted-Cut Objective}
\label{sec:lift}

We next construct a class in which a restricted but complete fiber tie has an
exact local cut description.  The result makes strict losses transparent and
gives compact implementations of the canonical optimum.

Let $H_0\in\F_2^{\rho\times n}$ be a full-row-rank parity-check matrix for a
base code $C_0=\ker H_0$.  Write a base word as $y=(a,z)$, where
$a\in\F_2$ and $z\in\F_2^{n-1}$.  Define the quotient map
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 Q&:\F_2^{n+1}\longrightarrow\F_2^n,\\
 Qx&=(x_1+x_2,x_3,\ldots,x_{n+1}),
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:Q}
\end{equation}
and set
\begin{equation}
 H=H_0Q.
 \label{eq:lift-H}
\end{equation}
The lifted code $C=\ker H$ contains $g=e_1+e_2$, and
$C/\langle g\rangle\cong C_0$.

Let $D_0$ be any deterministic base section with image $T_0$.  A binary tie
field $\tau$ on the selected words $(1,z)\in T_0$ defines
\begin{equation}
 L_\tau(a,z)=
 \begin{cases}
  (0,0,z),&a=0,\\
  (\tau(a,z),1+\tau(a,z),z),&a=1.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:tie-lift}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the first two bits are
\begin{equation}
 (a\tau(a,z),\,a+a\tau(a,z)).
 \label{eq:tie-prefix}
\end{equation}
The factors $a$ in~\eqref{eq:tie-prefix} are essential: on an $a=0$ fiber,
the prefix $00$ is the unique ML choice for $p<1/2$.

Let $\Gamma_1(T_0)$ be the graph with vertex set
\begin{equation}
 V_1=\{(1,z)\in T_0\},
\end{equation}
where two vertices are adjacent if they differ in one $z$ coordinate.  Give an
edge $e=\{y,y'\}$ weight
\begin{equation}
 w_e(q)=q^{\min\{\wt(y),\wt(y')\}}.
 \label{eq:cut-weight}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact weighted cut over the complete fiber-lift ML class]
\label{thm:weighted-cut}
For every tie field $\tau$, $L_\tau\circ D_0$ is a valid section of $H$.  If
$D_0$ is exact ML for the base code, every lifted section is exact ML for the
lifted code.  All tie fields choose a representative of the same weight in
every syndrome and satisfy
\begin{equation}
 W_\tau(q)=W_0(q),\qquad
 Z_\tau(p)=(1-p)Z_0(p).
 \label{eq:lift-mass}
\end{equation}
If $P_0(q)$ is the accepted-edge polynomial of the base section, then
\begin{align}
 P_\tau(q)&=P_0(q)-\Cut_\tau(q),
 \label{eq:lift-cut}\\
 \Cut_\tau(q)&=\sum_{e=\{y,y'\}\in E(\Gamma_1)}
 w_e(q)\ind\{\tau(y)\ne\tau(y')\}.
 \label{eq:cut}
\end{align}
A constant field is therefore globally DQI-optimal over the complete
pointwise-ML-equivalent class obtained from the fixed base section $D_0$.
The optimizers are exactly the fields that are constant on each connected
component of $\Gamma_1(T_0)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fiber $Q^{-1}(a,z)$ contains two words that differ by $g$.  For $a=0$,
their prefixes are $00$ and $11$, so $00$ is the unique fiberwise ML choice.
For $a=1$, their prefixes are $01$ and $10$, which tie.  In either case,
\begin{equation}
 \max_{Qx=y}\pi_p^{(n+1)}(x)=(1-p)\pi_p^{(n)}(y).
 \label{eq:fiber-prob}
\end{equation}
Thus a lifted base ML word is full-code ML and
$\wt(L_\tau(y))=\wt(y)$, proving~\eqref{eq:lift-mass}.

It remains to classify physical one-bit pairs.  A selected base edge in
coordinate $a$ lifts from $00$ to either $01$ or $10$ and remains a physical
edge.  A $z$ edge in the $a=0$ slice keeps prefix $00$ and also remains.  A
$z$ edge in the $a=1$ slice remains precisely when its endpoint labels agree.
If they disagree, the prefix changes from $01$ to $10$ while one $z$ bit also
changes, so the lifted distance is three.  Conversely, every physical unit
vector maps under $Q$ to a base unit vector, so no extra pair is created.
This proves~\eqref{eq:lift-cut}.  Since all cut weights are positive, zero cut
is attainable and optimal.  It is attained exactly by fields constant on each
connected component.
\end{proof}

The scope in Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-cut} matters.  The result is global over
all choices of the $01/10$ ambiguity above the fixed base ML section.  It does
not assert optimality over arbitrary non-ML transversals or over ML base
sections outside this fiber-lift class.

\subsection{Cover, gauge, and algorithmic interpretations}

The tie graph gives a literal two-sheet covering graph: lift $v\in V_1$ to
$(v,b)$, $b\in\F_2$, and connect equal labels above every base edge.  A tie
field is a section of the cover, and~\eqref{eq:cut} is the cover-edge weight
lost by that section.  A constant field fixes one sheet globally.  With
several binary tie coordinates and translation constraints
$u_t=u_s+\delta_{st}$, the label group becomes $\F_2^t$ and the problem is
Abelian Max-2Lin.  The cycle sum of $\delta_{st}$ is the cover holonomy.  A
vertex relabeling gauges all offsets to zero exactly when every cycle sum
vanishes.

The aligned lifted decoder has a particularly compact implementation:
\begin{equation}
 s\xmapsto{D_0}(a,z)\longmapsto(0,a,z).
 \label{eq:aligned-algorithm}
\end{equation}
It calls the base decoder once and performs $O(n)$ reversible bit operations.
No exponential syndrome table, fractional-programming loop, or complex
message is involved.  In a general unfrustrated Abelian lift, choose one root
label per component and propagate offsets.  For $L=2^t$ labels, translation
constraints reduce exact tree max-sum from $O(|E|L^2)$ to $O(|E|L)$ by keeping
the largest and second-largest incoming values.

\section{Reliable Connected Families}
\label{sec:reliable}

The weighted-cut identity would have limited operational force if the common
postselection probability vanished.  The next result rules out that failure
mode.

\begin{corollary}[Any reliable base section gives a constant gap]
\label{cor:reliable}
Let $\{D_{0,n}\}$ be deterministic base sections with success probabilities
$Z_{0,n}=1-\varepsilon_n$, where $\varepsilon_n\to0$.  In the quotient lift,
compare a constant tie field with
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\rm par}(1,z)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n-1}z_j.
 \label{eq:parity-field}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
 Z_{\rm const}=Z_{\rm par}
  &=(1-p)Z_{0,n}\longrightarrow1-p,
 \label{eq:reliable-Z}\\
 \mathcal J_{\rm const}-\mathcal J_{\rm par}
  &\longrightarrow p\sqrt{p(1-p)},
 \label{eq:reliable-J}\\
 R_{\rm const}-R_{\rm par}
  &\longrightarrow[p(1-p)]^{3/2}.
 \label{eq:reliable-R}
\end{align}
Consequently, the expected number of independent postselection attempts
$1/Z$ tends to $1/(1-p)$.  If amplitude amplification is available, its
repetition factor $1/\sqrt Z$ tends separately to $1/\sqrt{1-p}$, before the
cost of the reversible decoder oracle is charged.
No ML property is needed for these limits.  If $D_{0,n}$ is exact ML, both
lifted sections are exact ML by Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-cut}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:reliable-Z} follows from~\eqref{eq:lift-mass}.  Parity
changes on every $z$ edge, so it cuts every accepted edge of the $a=1$ slice.
For each of the $n-1$ outside coordinates, the complete $a=1$ edge mass equals
$p\sqrt{p(1-p)}$.  Relative to the complete slice, a pair is missing only if
at least one base endpoint is a decoding failure.  With $q=p/(1-p)$, the
missing normalized mass in one coordinate is at most
$(\sqrt q+1/\sqrt q)\varepsilon_n$.  Summing over the outside coordinates,
dividing by the lifted length $n+1$, and using $Z_{0,n}\to1$ proves
\eqref{eq:reliable-J}.  Multiplication by the common acceptance proves
\eqref{eq:reliable-R}.
\end{proof}

The lift also transfers rate and decoding complexity exactly.  A base
$[n,k_n]$ code becomes an $[n+1,k_n+1]$ code, so its rate has the same limit.
If $D_{0,n}$ runs in time $T(n)$, both lifted sections run in
$T(n)+O(n)$ time.  Their selected weights and acceptance are identical to
each other; the common full-code acceptance is $(1-p)Z_{0,n}$, not
$Z_{0,n}$.  Hence any reliable positive-rate decoder family gives a
positive-rate strict DQI separation.  Exact ML status transfers when the base
decoder itself is exact ML.

Taking an odd repetition code as the base in Corollary~\ref{cor:reliable}
already gives a connected degree-three realization, but the quotient lift
contains the weight-two word $e_1+e_2$.  The next family removes this artifact
and gives the principal explicit witness.

\begin{theorem}[Indecomposable fixed-distance strict-gain family]
\label{thm:twisted-family}
Fix even $d\ge4$ and odd $n\ge d-1$, and put $N=d+n$.  On coordinates
$(x_1,\ldots,x_d,z_1,\ldots,z_n)$, define
\begin{equation}
 g=(\ind^d,0^n),\qquad h=(e_1,\ind^n),\qquad
 C_{d,n}=\operatorname{span}\{g,h\}.
 \label{eq:twisted-code}
\end{equation}
For the inner repetition code $\operatorname{span}\{\ind^d\}$, let $A_0$
and $A_1$ choose the unique lighter member of every antipodal pair off the
middle layer.  On a middle-layer pair, $A_b$ chooses the member whose first
bit is $b$.  Let
\begin{equation}
 T_n=\{z\in\F_2^n:\wt(z)\le(n-1)/2\},
 \label{eq:outer-ball}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{align}
 T_{\rm const}&=A_0\times T_n,
 \label{eq:twisted-const}\\
 T_{\rm par}&=\{(x,z):z\in T_n,
                  x\in A_{\wt(z)\bmod2}\}.
 \label{eq:twisted-par}
\end{align}
Both sets are exact ML transversals of $C_{d,n}$ and select a word of the
same weight in every coset.  Moreover, $T_{\rm const}$ is downward closed and
therefore globally DQI-optimal over all deterministic ML sections.

For the Bernoulli profile, put
\begin{align}
 t_d(p)&=\frac12\binom d{d/2}[p(1-p)]^{d/2},
 \label{eq:td}\\
 \zeta_d(p)&=\sum_{j<d/2}\binom djp^j(1-p)^{d-j}+t_d(p),
 \label{eq:zeta-d}\\
 Z_n^{\rm rep}&=\sum_{j=0}^{(n-1)/2}\binom njp^j(1-p)^{n-j},
 \label{eq:outer-Z}\\
 E_n^{\rm rep}&=\sum_{j=0}^{(n-3)/2}(n-j)\binom nj
 p^{j+1/2}(1-p)^{n-j-1/2}.
 \label{eq:outer-E}
\end{align}
The two sections have common acceptance $\zeta_dZ_n^{\rm rep}$ and exact
accepted-edge separation
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{\rm const}-\mathcal E_{\rm par}
 =t_d E_n^{\rm rep}.
 \label{eq:twisted-exact-gap}
\end{equation}
For fixed $d$ and $0<p<1/2$, as odd $n\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal J_{\rm const}-\mathcal J_{\rm par}
 &\longrightarrow\frac{t_d(p)}{\zeta_d(p)}\sqrt{p(1-p)},
 \label{eq:twisted-J-limit}\\
 R_{\rm const}-R_{\rm par}
 &\longrightarrow t_d(p)\sqrt{p(1-p)}.
 \label{eq:twisted-R-limit}
\end{align}
The code has minimum distance $d$, nonzero distinct parity-check columns, a
connected check representation of maximum degree three, and linear-time exact
ML decoding.  It is coordinate-indecomposable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each $A_b$ is a transversal of $\operatorname{span}\{\ind^d\}$, and $T_n$
contains one word from every outer antipodal pair.  Hence neither selected set
contains two words differing by $g$, $h$, or $g+h$; its cardinality is
$2^{N-2}$, so it is a transversal of~\eqref{eq:twisted-code}.

Every selected $(x,z)$ satisfies $\wt(x)\le d/2$ and
$\wt(z)\le(n-1)/2$.  Adding $g$, $h$, and $g+h$ changes its weight by,
respectively,
\begin{align}
 \Delta_g&=d-2\wt(x),\nonumber\\
 \Delta_h&=(1-2x_1)+n-2\wt(z),\nonumber\\
 \Delta_{g+h}&=d-1-2(\wt(x)-x_1)+n-2\wt(z).
 \label{eq:twisted-ml-differences}
\end{align}
All three quantities are nonnegative.  Thus both sections are exact ML.
Their inner representatives have equal weight syndrome by syndrome, which
also proves pointwise weight equivalence.

The mass selected by either inner section is $\zeta_d$, and the mass of its
selected middle layer is $t_d$.  Along an accepted outer edge, the parity rule
switches the inner representative exactly on these middle-layer ties and
destroys that one-bit pair; the constant rule preserves it.  The inner-edge
masses of $A_0$ and $A_1$ are equal because every selected middle-layer word
has exactly $d/2$ selected downward neighbors.  Product factorization then
gives~\eqref{eq:twisted-exact-gap}.  Also,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{E_n^{\rm rep}}n=\sqrt{p(1-p)}
 \Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n-1,p)\le(n-3)/2\},
 \label{eq:outer-E-limit}
\end{equation}
so $Z_n^{\rm rep}\to1$ and $E_n^{\rm rep}/n\to\sqrt{p(1-p)}$.
Equations~\eqref{eq:twisted-J-limit} and~\eqref{eq:twisted-R-limit} follow.

The sets $A_0$ and $T_n$ are downward closed, as is their product
$T_{\rm const}$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-ml} proves global optimality.
The sparse checks
\begin{equation}
 x_2=\cdots=x_d,\qquad z_1=\cdots=z_n,\qquad
 x_1+x_2+z_1=0
 \label{eq:twisted-checks}
\end{equation}
have rank $N-2$, maximum degree three, and a connected Tanner graph.  The
three nonzero codeword weights are $d$, $n+1$, and $n+d-1$, so
$d_{\min}=d$.  This excludes zero and repeated parity-check columns.  Every
pair among $g,h,g+h$ has intersecting support; a decomposable binary
dimension-two code would have a disjoint-support basis.  Finally, an arbitrary
coset representative has only four codeword shifts.  Comparing their weights
and applying the stated tie rule gives exact ML decoding in $O(N)$ time.
\end{proof}

The fixed $d$ is substantive: Stirling's formula gives
$t_d=\Theta([2\sqrt{p(1-p)}]^d/\sqrt d)$.  Thus any chosen fixed even
distance retains a strict constant gap, whereas this particular gap vanishes
if $d$ grows.  The theorem is an exact-ML structural witness, not a
capacity-achieving ensemble or a generic quantum-advantage claim.

\subsection{A positive-rate polynomial-time feasibility family}

The preceding indecomposable family has vanishing rate.  The following
complementary construction shows that positive rate, polynomial-time exact ML,
nonvanishing acceptance, and a strict DQI separation can coexist.  Its
logarithmic block decomposition is deliberately explicit; it is a feasibility
result, not a claim about natural LDPC ensembles.

\begin{theorem}[Positive-rate polynomial-time exact-ML separation]
\label{thm:positive-rate-family}
Fix $0<p<1/2$, a target channel-code rate
$0<R<1-h_2(p)$, and an even integer $d\ge4$.  There exist binary linear codes
$\widetilde C_N$ and two deterministic exact-ML sections
$D_N^{\rm const},D_N^{\rm par}$ with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\dim(\widetilde C_N)/N\to R$ and
$d_{\min}(\widetilde C_N)=d$ for all sufficiently large $N$.  A full-rank
parity-check representation has nonzero distinct columns, a connected Tanner
graph, $O(N\log N)$ nonzero entries, and maximum row weight $O(\log N)$.
\item Both sections run in $N^{O(1)}$ time and select representatives of the
same weight syndrome by syndrome.  Their common acceptance satisfies
\begin{equation}
 Z_N^{\rm const}=Z_N^{\rm par}\longrightarrow\zeta_d(p)>0.
 \label{eq:positive-rate-Z}
\end{equation}
\item In the phase-averaged binary accepted-pair model,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal J_N^{\rm const}-\mathcal J_N^{\rm par}
 &\longrightarrow \frac{t_d(p)}{\zeta_d(p)}\sqrt{p(1-p)}>0,
 \label{eq:positive-rate-J}\\
 R_N^{\rm const}-R_N^{\rm par}
 &\longrightarrow t_d(p)\sqrt{p(1-p)}>0.
 \label{eq:positive-rate-R}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
For every fixed $\eta>0$, $d$ can be chosen once so that
$\zeta_d(p)>1-\eta$; the two limits in
\eqref{eq:positive-rate-J}--\eqref{eq:positive-rate-R} then remain positive
constants as $N\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose $\tau\in(p,1/2)$ with $h_2(\tau)<1-R$, and choose
$\delta>0$ with $h_2(\delta)<1-R$.  For a block length $b$, draw a binary
$\rho_b\times b$ matrix with
$\rho_b=\lceil(1-R)b\rceil$.  For an error $e$ of weight at most $\tau b$,
the collision union bound gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\Pr_H\{\exists e'\ne e:\ He'=He,\ \wt(e')\le\wt(e)\}\\
 &\hspace{12mm}\le 2^{-\rho_b}\sum_{j=0}^{\wt(e)}\binom bj.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:log-block-collision}
\end{equation}
Together with the Bernoulli tail above $\tau b$, this shows that the expected
ML block-error probability is at most $\exp(-2\kappa b)$ for some
$\kappa=\kappa(p,R)>0$ and all sufficiently large $b$.  Also,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr_H\{d_{\min}(\ker H)<\delta b\}
 \le 2^{-\rho_b}\sum_{j<\delta b}\binom bj=\exp[-\Omega(b)].
 \label{eq:log-block-distance}
\end{equation}
A random rectangular matrix is full row rank with probability tending to one.
Markov's inequality at level $\exp(-\kappa b)$ therefore selects full-row-rank
block matrices $H_b$ for which the ML block-error probability is at most
$\exp(-\kappa b)$ and the distance bound holds.

Let $L\to\infty$, put $b_L=\lceil A\log L\rceil$ with fixed
$A>1/\kappa$, and take the direct sum of $L$ copies of $\ker H_{b_L}$.
Its exact ML decoder acts independently on the blocks.  The outer success
probability $Z_{0,L}$ obeys
\begin{equation}
 1-Z_{0,L}\le L\exp(-\kappa b_L)\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:log-block-success}
\end{equation}
Exhaustive ML decoding of one block costs $2^{O(b_L)}$; over all blocks this is
polynomial in the outer length $n_L=Lb_L$.  The outer rate tends to $R$ and
its minimum distance is at least $\delta b_L$.

Now take the direct product with the fixed inner repetition code
$\operatorname{span}\{\ind^d\}$.  Use $A_0$ on every inner antipodal coset for
$D_N^{\rm const}$.  For $D_N^{\rm par}$, replace $A_0$ by $A_1$ on the inner
middle-layer ties exactly when the selected outer representative has odd
Hamming weight.  Direct-product ML factorization proves that both are exact ML,
and their selected weights agree in every full syndrome.  The common
acceptance is $\zeta_d Z_{0,L}$.

Let $E_{0,L}$ be the accepted-edge mass of the outer section.  Switching the
inner orientation destroys precisely the outer accepted edges paired with an
inner middle-layer representative.  The inner edge masses of $A_0$ and $A_1$
are equal, so the finite identity is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_N^{\rm const}-\mathcal E_N^{\rm par}=t_d(p)E_{0,L}.
 \label{eq:positive-rate-finite-gap}
\end{equation}
The complete $n_L$-cube has edge mass
$n_L\sqrt{p(1-p)}$.  Every edge absent from the selected outer set touches a
failure word; summing incident edge mass at such words gives
\begin{equation}
 0\le n_L\sqrt{p(1-p)}-E_{0,L}
 \le \frac{n_L(1-Z_{0,L})}{\sqrt{p/(1-p)}}.
 \label{eq:outer-edge-reliability}
\end{equation}
Hence $E_{0,L}/n_L\to\sqrt{p(1-p)}$.  Since $N=n_L+d$,
\eqref{eq:positive-rate-Z}--\eqref{eq:positive-rate-R} follow.

The full code has dimension $1+L(b_L-\rho_{b_L})$, rate tending to $R$, and
minimum distance $\min\{d,\delta b_L\}=d$.  Minimum distance at least four
excludes zero and repeated parity-check columns.  Start from any full-rank
block-diagonal parity-check matrix.  In each Tanner component choose one row
$r_j$ and replace the selected rows by
$r_0,r_0+r_1,r_1+r_2,\ldots$.  This is an invertible row operation, connects
all components, and raises row weight by at most a factor of two.  Because
$b_L=O(\log N)$, the resulting representation has the claimed sparsity.
Finally, $\zeta_d(p)\to1$ as even $d\to\infty$ by a binomial-tail bound,
while $t_d(p)>0$ for every finite $d$.
\end{proof}

\section{How Much Can ML Tie-Breaking Change the DQI Score?}
\label{sec:ambiguity}

The quotient lift introduces persistent weight-two ambiguity.  We next show
that a nonvanishing selected ambiguous mass relative to acceptance is necessary
for an asymptotic conditional-score gap.

For an ML decoder, the mass of one selected representative from syndrome $s$
is unambiguous because every element of $\mathcal L_s$ has the same weight.
Define the total selected ML mass on ambiguous syndromes
\begin{equation}
 A_{\rm tie}:=\sum_{s:\,|\mathcal L_s|>1}\pi_p(e_s),
 \qquad e_s\in\mathcal L_s.
 \label{eq:tie-mass}
\end{equation}
For two deterministic ML sections $D,D'$, define the one-sided changed mass
\begin{equation}
 \alpha(D,D'):=\sum_{s:\,D(s)\ne D'(s)}\pi_p(D(s)).
 \label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[DQI diameter of the ML tie class]
\label{thm:tie-diameter}
Let $D,D'$ be deterministic ML sections of the same code, and let $Z$ be
their common acceptance.  Then
\begin{align}
 \alpha(D,D')&\le A_{\rm tie}\le1-Z,
 \label{eq:alpha-order}\\
 |\mathcal J_D-\mathcal J_{D'}|
 &\le \frac{\alpha(D,D')}{Z\sqrt q}
 =\frac{\alpha(D,D')}{Z}\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p}}.
 \label{eq:diameter-pair}
\end{align}
Consequently, the diameter of the complete deterministic ML tie class obeys
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{diam}(\mathcal J_{\rm ML})
 \le\frac{A_{\rm tie}}{Z}\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p}}
 \le\frac{1-Z}{Z}\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p}}.
 \label{eq:diameter}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first inequality in~\eqref{eq:alpha-order} follows because two ML rules
can differ only at an ambiguous syndrome.  At each such syndrome, at least one
unselected ML representative has the same probability as the selected one.
These unselected representatives are decoding failures, so their total mass
is at most $1-Z$.  This proves the second inequality.

Suppose first that $\mathcal E_D\ge\mathcal E_{D'}$ and put
$\Delta_+=T_D\setminus T_{D'}$.  Equal ML weights give
$\pi_p(\Delta_+)=\alpha(D,D')$.  Every edge counted by $T_D$ but not by
$T_{D'}$ has an endpoint in $\Delta_+$.  Charge such an edge to one such
endpoint.  If $\wt(e)=j$, the total
geometric-amplitude mass of all $m$ incident edges is
\begin{equation}
 \pi_p(e)\left(\frac{j}{\sqrt q}+(m-j)\sqrt q\right)
 \le \frac{m\pi_p(e)}{\sqrt q},
 \label{eq:incident-bound}
\end{equation}
where $0<q<1$.  Summing~\eqref{eq:incident-bound} over $\Delta_+$ and dividing
by $mZ$ proves~\eqref{eq:diameter-pair}.  If the ordering of the two edge
masses is reversed, exchange $D$ and $D'$.  Maximization over $D,D'$ gives
\eqref{eq:diameter}.
\end{proof}

In particular, for fixed $p\in(0,1/2)$, if $A_{\rm tie}=o(Z)$, every
deterministic ML tie rule has the same asymptotic DQI score.  The stronger,
simpler condition $Z\to1$ also forces the diameter to zero.  The reliable
quotient lift avoids this no-gain regime because its
common acceptance tends to $1-p$, not to one, and its controlled fiber tie has
constant probability mass.

\subsection{A secondary weight-enumerator estimate}

The following classical estimate relates ambiguity to the code spectrum.  If
$c\in C\setminus\{0\}$ has weight $d$, then $e$ and $e+c$ have equal weight
only when $d$ is even and $e$ has $d/2$ ones on $\operatorname{supp}(c)$.
Under $\BSC(p)$ this event has probability
\begin{align}
 B_d(p)&=\ind\{d\text{ even}\}\binom{d}{d/2}
 [p(1-p)]^{d/2},\\
 B_d(p)&\le \theta(p)^d,\qquad
 \theta(p)=2\sqrt{p(1-p)}<1.
 \label{eq:bhattacharyya}
\end{align}
A union bound yields
\begin{equation}
 \alpha(D,D')\le
 \sum_{c\in C\setminus\{0\}}B_{\wt(c)}(p)
 \le\sum_{c\in C\setminus\{0\}}\theta(p)^{\wt(c)}.
 \label{eq:weight-envelope}
\end{equation}
This is a sufficient classical envelope, not the principal novelty.  It can be
looser than~\eqref{eq:alpha-order}, especially for reliable decoders.  For an
$[m,k,d_{\min}]$ family, the simple condition
$2^k\theta(p)^{d_{\min}}\to0$ makes the right-hand side vanish.

\section{A Secondary DE-NFG Evaluator and the Role of Bethe Analysis}
\label{sec:denfg}

The pair functional is naturally a two-copy contraction.  This section makes
that statement exact and separates it from any approximation claim.

For a deterministic decoder $D$, define the unnormalized accepted vector
\begin{equation}
 |v_D\rangle=\sum_{e\in\F_2^m}
 p^{\wt(e)/2}(1-p)^{(m-\wt(e))/2}A_D(e)|e\rangle.
 \label{eq:accepted-vector}
\end{equation}
Let $E_{D,i}$ denote the undirected geometric-amplitude mass of accepted
hypercube edges in coordinate $i$, so that
$\sum_iE_{D,i}=mR_D$.

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic decoder circuit to a strict DE-NFG]
\label{thm:denfg}
Suppose $D$ is represented by a finite deterministic circuit with a unique
internal-wire assignment for every input.  Double every wire and replace a
local Boolean indicator $\phi_g(u)$ by
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_g(u,u')=\phi_g(u)\phi_g(u').
 \label{eq:doubled-gate}
\end{equation}
The resulting strict DE-NFG has closed contractions
\begin{align}
 Z_D&=\langle v_D|v_D\rangle,
 \label{eq:denfg-Z}\\
 C_{D,i}&=\langle v_D|(I+X_i)|v_D\rangle
          =Z_D+2E_{D,i}.
 \label{eq:denfg-C}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 R_D=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i=1}^m(C_{D,i}-Z_D).
 \label{eq:denfg-R}
\end{equation}
Every doubled deterministic local factor in~\eqref{eq:doubled-gate} is
rank-one positive semidefinite.  The Bernoulli boundary is an outer product,
and the inserted boundary matrix $I+X_i$ is real, entrywise nonnegative, and
positive semidefinite.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the doubled deterministic circuit produces
$A_D(e)A_D(e')$ with the two Bernoulli amplitudes in
\eqref{eq:accepted-vector}.  Closing corresponding wires with the identity
sets $e'=e$ and gives~\eqref{eq:denfg-Z}.  The $X_i$ term sets
$e'=e+\mathbf e_i$.  Both orientations of every undirected accepted edge are present,
which gives the factor two in~\eqref{eq:denfg-C}.  Summing the coordinate
identities yields~\eqref{eq:denfg-R}.  Finally,
$\Phi_g=\phi_g\phi_g^T$ and the Bernoulli boundary are outer products, while
$I+X_i=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ on bit $i$.
These observations prove the stated positivity properties.
\end{proof}

The phase-averaged binary compilation is therefore real.  Complex messages
carry no additional exact information for this contraction.  Before averaging
a fixed binary instance, signed real factors can remain; over nonbinary
alphabets, group characters can be genuinely complex.  Neither case implies
a decoding gain without a uniform algorithm and matched computational cost.

\subsection{Exact and approximate inference boundaries}

If the compiled normal factor graph is acyclic, one inward and one outward SPA
sweep evaluates the relevant contractions exactly~\cite{kschischang2001}.
If its treewidth is $w$ and the original wire alphabet has size $d$, exact
elimination is polynomial in network size and exponential in the doubled
width, with a representative factor $d^{2(w+1)}$.  A private auxiliary tree
whose exact contraction equals one original factor merely changes the
elimination order.  After a complete internal sweep it produces the same
boundary update, the same partition function, and the same decoder.  Such a
refinement cannot create a strict gain by itself.

On a loopy compilation, the Bethe partition function and its stationary
points supply approximations to the exact contractions.  Every factor in the
phase-averaged construction above is entrywise nonnegative.  Treating each
paired variable as one enlarged-alphabet variable therefore makes it an
ordinary nonnegative factor graph, to which the standard finite-cover
characterization applies~\cite{vontobel2013}.  No general complex DE-NFG cover
theorem is needed here.  For genuinely complex DE-NFGs, the analogous theorem
is proved under a stated sufficient condition, while the unrestricted
statement remains a conjecture~\cite{huang2025}.  We use the present network as
an exact offline evaluator on trees or bounded-width graphs and treat loopy
Bethe quantities as candidate surrogates.  A surrogate earns an
algorithmic claim only after it predicts decoder rankings more accurately or
more cheaply than exact paired sampling in~\eqref{eq:paired-sampling}.

\subsection{When one copy is enough}

The universal optimizer in Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-ml} satisfies
$P(q)=W'_{\rm ML}(q)$.  Hence a single-copy partition function and its
derivative determine the optimal ML pair term; no doubled graph is needed if
$W_{\rm ML}$ is already available.  The DE-NFG remains useful for a restricted
or non-ML decoder, whose exact loss is the two-point downward defect in
\eqref{eq:down-defect}.  Paired sampling provides the direct statistical
baseline in either case.

\section{Algorithms}
\label{sec:algorithms}

The theorems yield exact targets and compact algorithms at different levels
of structure.

\subsection{Canonical ML section}

For unequal bit probabilities, compare candidates by the ordered metric
$(L,-G_\gamma,\text{monomial-order key})$ from~\eqref{eq:product-rule}; retain
the first word with each new syndrome.  When the probabilities and coordinate
rewards are both uniform, this reduces to Hamming weight followed by the fixed
monomial order.  The resulting table is the
globally DQI-optimal ML section from Theorems~\ref{thm:asymmetric-optimum} and
\ref{thm:universal-ml}.  An exact ML trellis or junction-tree solver can carry
$-G_\gamma$ and the word key as secondary metrics, and an ML list decoder can apply
the same rule to its minimum-likelihood candidates.  The full table can be
exponential in the codimension, so this is not a general polynomial-time ML
construction.  For
$C_{d,n}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:twisted-family}, the same canonical rule reduces
to four weight comparisons and a deterministic tie test, hence runs in
$O(N)$ time.

\subsection{Aligned quotient-lift decoder}

Given a base decoder $D_0$, the optimal member of the fixed-base fiber-lift ML
class is the aligned rule in~\eqref{eq:aligned-algorithm}.  It preserves the
base representative weight syndrome by syndrome.  Its cost is one base
decoder call plus $O(n)$ bit operations, and its reversible implementation
uses the same asymptotic work.  The competing parity field in
\eqref{eq:parity-field} also costs $O(n)$; hence the separation in
Section~\ref{sec:reliable} is matched in decoding order and pointwise ML
likelihood.

\subsection{Restricted optimization on an explicit candidate graph}

When an architecture restricts the available candidates, construct the
pairwise compatibility rewards in~\eqref{eq:general-max-section}.  On a tree,
a max-sum message from syndrome
$s$ to $t$ is
\begin{equation}
 M_{s\to t}(b)=
 \sum_{u\in N(s)\setminus t}
 \max_{a\in\mathcal L_u}
 \{M_{u\to s}(a)+\psi_{us}(a,b)\},
 \label{eq:maxsum}
\end{equation}
where $b\in\mathcal L_s$ and $\psi_{us}$ is the weighted one-bit
compatibility indicator.  Backtracking returns the exact section.  A junction
tree gives the bounded-treewidth complexities stated after
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-maximum-section}.  For two-label full matchings,
offset propagation solves the zero-frustration case.  For the quotient lift,
the offsets vanish and the aligned field is immediate.

\subsection{Stochastic selection among compact decoder architectures}

When the full candidate graph is exponential, restrict attention to a compact
family $\{D_\vartheta\}$, such as equivalent parity-check representations,
redundant-check choices, message schedules, or damping parameters.  If all
candidates output minimum-weight representatives, $Z$ is exactly common and
the design objective is simply $R_{D_\vartheta}$.  Proposition
\ref{prop:paired-sampling} supplies a direct unbiased estimator:

\begin{enumerate}
\item sample a coordinate and the remaining Bernoulli error bits;
\item run $D_\vartheta$ on the two forced-bit syndromes;
\item record one if both returned representatives equal their inputs;
\item average and multiply by $\sqrt{p(1-p)}$.
\end{enumerate}

The same random pairs can evaluate all candidates, reducing comparison
variance.  If the candidate family allows different acceptance, estimate $Z$
separately and report both $\mathcal J$ and $R$; optimizing a conditional score
alone can conceal an exponential retry penalty.  A Bethe or DE-NFG surrogate
is useful only in a regime where exact pair sampling is too costly and the
surrogate's ranking is validated at matched compute.

\section{Reproducible Numerical Audit}
\label{sec:numerics}

The numerical study certifies the asymmetric and radial optima, the
reliability--sensitivity identity, the robust strict-gain family, and the
quotient-lift identities; it also tests which
broader algorithmic claims are supported.  All reported decoders are
deterministic and classical.  The main experiments use real arithmetic; no BP
with quantum messages is included.  Machine-readable outputs, deterministic
scripts, and a compact command list accompany this draft.

\subsection{Asymmetric-product certificate}

An exact-rational program tested Theorem~\ref{thm:asymmetric-optimum} on 20
seeded random codes of lengths five through seven and five unequal
log-likelihood patterns, each paired with unequal positive coordinate
rewards.  It chose $r_i=2^{w_i}$ and
$p_i=1/(1+4^{w_i})$, so every probability and edge mass was rational.
Every deterministic ML section was enumerated, with as many as 384 sections
in one test.  Every case passed downward closure of the canonical rule, the
edge-charge identity~\eqref{eq:asym-edge}, the two-term defect
\eqref{eq:asym-defect}, and global optimality.

\subsection{Reliability--sensitivity certificate}

An exact-rational implementation independently computed $Z_{\rm ML}$, its
active one-sided directional derivative, the optimal accepted-edge mass, and
the radial derivative and area identities.  It passed 237 radial identities
and 79 asymmetric directional identities.  A separate adversarial enumerator
checked 4,974 syndrome maps and 96,704 asymmetric ML sections, including
rank-deficient representations, zero columns, repeated columns, and parameters
near both open endpoints.  It also found explicit counterexamples when the
assumptions $p_i<1/2$ or $\gamma_i>0$ are removed.

\subsection{Coefficientwise optimum certificate}

An independent exhaustive program enumerated every deterministic ML section
for the $[5,2]$ code in~\eqref{eq:five-code} and for 16 seeded random full-
rank parity-check matrices of lengths five through seven.  Individual tests
contained up to 3072 ML sections.  In every case, it verified
$N_k(T)\le k n_k$ coefficient by coefficient, the exact layer-defect
identity, downward closure of the graded-lexicographic section, and
$N_k(T_{\rm glex})=k n_k$ for all $k$.  At a positive test value of $q$, the
enumerated maximizers were exactly the downward-closed ML sections.

\subsection{Linear-source threshold certificate}

An independent exact-arithmetic program checked the finite bridge used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:linear-shannon-dqi}.  On 312 seeded full-rank maps of lengths
four through ten, it verified transversal cardinality, downward closure, the
accepted-edge/conditional-weight identity, and the top-mass converse.  It
also exhaustively enumerated every binary matrix in 104 small collision tests
and verified the random-syndrome union bound, including rank-deficient maps.
Exact rational checks confirmed the exponent derivative and DQI-deficit
algebra above threshold.

\subsection{Indecomposable-family certificate}

Exact enumeration checked Theorem~\ref{thm:twisted-family} for
$d\in\{4,6\}$ and three odd outer lengths beginning at $d-1$, at $p=0.24$.
It verified the full coset partition, pointwise ML equality, minimum distance,
rank, distinct columns, Tanner connectedness, indecomposability, and
\eqref{eq:twisted-exact-gap}; the largest displayed edge-identity residual was
$3.1\times10^{-14}$.  At $n=401$, the $d=4$ conditional gap was $0.049372$
against the limit $0.049865$, and the acceptance-weighted per-attempt gap was
$0.042206$ against $0.042627$.  The corresponding $d=6$ values were
$0.028161$ against $0.028582$ and $0.025535$ against $0.025917$.

\subsection{Positive-rate feasibility certificate}

An independent exact certificate pairs the inner $\operatorname{Rep}_4$
gadget with the self-dual $[8,4,4]$ $\operatorname{RM}(1,3)$ outer code.  It
exhaustively verifies pointwise exact-ML equality, the coefficientwise version
of~\eqref{eq:positive-rate-finite-gap}, minimum distance four, nonzero distinct
columns, and a connected row-equivalent check representation.  A separate
exponent audit evaluates~\eqref{eq:log-block-collision} and
\eqref{eq:log-block-distance} for a representative parameter choice and checks
that the logarithmic-block union bound decreases to zero.  These computations
test the finite algebra and constants; the asymptotic existence statement in
Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-rate-family} follows from its analytic proof.

\subsection{Quotient-lift certificate}

\subsection{Negative controls}

Three controls delimit the interpretation.

\begin{enumerate}
\item A diagonal DE-NFG embedding reproduces ordinary real BP.  A zero-phase
rank-one amplitude parameterization reduces to a real prior-temperature
rescaling.  Neither produced a complex-specific gain against strengthened
real baselines.

\item For a fixed binary DQI offset, the exact factors can be signed, but
instance-tailored decoder selection was both slower and worse than directly
optimizing the corresponding classical Max-XOR objective in the tested
sizes.  An unrestricted instance-aware decoder can encode a solution that was
computed beforehand; its design cost cannot be omitted.

\item Loopy Bethe/max-sum was exact after polishing in the five-bit example,
but its local ranking correlation varied by code and could be poor.  Direct
paired evaluation was cheaper whenever all syndromes could be enumerated.
\end{enumerate}

These controls motivate the bounded DE-NFG claim in
Section~\ref{sec:denfg}: exact compilation is proved, while a general loopy
approximation advantage remains open.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What conventional decoder metrics miss}

The quotient lift holds fixed every one-point statistic normally used to
compare deterministic ML tie rules.  The representative weight is identical
for every syndrome, so the likelihood, frame-success probability, complete
Hamming-layer profile, and expected retry count are identical.  Nevertheless,
the pair term changes by a weighted cut.  The missing statistic is therefore
not a finer shell average; it is adjacency among selected representatives.
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-ml} sharpens this distinction: the common shell
profile determines the largest attainable pair term, but it does not determine
the value of an arbitrary tie rule.  The downward defect measures exactly how
far that rule lies below the shell-determined ceiling.
This distinction complements recent work that sharpens shell-weighted DQI
bounds~\cite{gao2026}.  A shell analysis and a pair analysis answer different
questions.

\subsection{When graph covers help}

Two cover structures appear and need not coincide.  First, an ML tie field can
select a section of a covering graph when its candidate compatibilities are
full bijections.  This is the decoder-design problem.  Second, finite covers
of a factor graph can characterize or approximate a Bethe partition function.
This is an inference problem.  The quotient lift solves the first problem
exactly by gauge alignment.  The DE-NFG compilation maps the DQI pair term to
the second setting.  Treating the two uses as distinct avoids the unsupported
claim that a graph transformation automatically improves BP.

\subsection{Implications for code design}

The ambiguity bound identifies a tension.  Large distance suppresses ML ties,
which is favorable for ordinary reliable decoding but leaves little room for
objective-aware tie-breaking.  The quotient lift decouples the two roles: an
arbitrary reliable base supplies error correction, while one weight-two fiber
supplies controlled ambiguity.  More general designs may distribute a small
number of low-weight tie fibers through a reliable code and optimize their
holonomy.  The twisted family in Theorem~\ref{thm:twisted-family} shows that a
strict gap does not require weight-two codewords, repeated columns, or an
algebraic direct product.  Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-rate-family} separately
shows that positive rate, polynomial-time exact ML, nonvanishing acceptance,
and a strict gap are mutually compatible, albeit in a logarithmic-block
feasibility construction.  Theorem~\ref{thm:tie-diameter} gives a necessary
mass budget, and Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-ml} gives the exact ML ceiling.

The open algorithmic problem is not to prove that BP is universally optimal.
It is to realize the ordered rule $(L,-G_\gamma,\text{key})$ compactly on useful
code families when exact ML itself is costly.  When both bit probabilities and
coordinate rewards are uniform, this is minimum weight followed by the fixed
monomial order.  Trellis,
junction-tree, and list decoders can carry these secondary keys exactly.  For
loopy approximate decoders, equivalent factor graphs and schedules can be
ranked against the canonical ceiling.  A DE-NFG Bethe approximation remains
an offline surrogate, with Proposition~\ref{prop:paired-sampling} as the
direct statistical baseline.

\subsection{Quantum-algorithm boundary}

The paper improves one component of a DQI implementation: the coherent effect
of a classical syndrome decoder.  The result establishes a strict state-quality
separation at matched classical decoding success and cost.  It does not by
itself establish quantum supremacy, an end-to-end speedup, or an advantage on
generic Max-XOR instances.  Those claims additionally require an instance
family, a complete reversible resource analysis, and comparison with the best
classical optimizer.  Extensions of DQI to weighted objectives and other
association schemes broaden the possible application domain
\cite{bu2026,krajenbrink2026}, while known structural obstructions reinforce
the need for instance-specific evidence~\cite{anschuetz2025}.

\section{Conclusion}

Maximum-likelihood ties are classically invisible to frame success but can be
visible to the phase-averaged binary DQI accepted-pair objective.  For
independent probabilities $0<p_i<1/2$, positive coordinate rewards, and
deterministic exact-ML sections, the ordered rule based on $L$, $G_\gamma$, and
a multiplicative monomial order is globally optimal.  Its exact defect splits
into a fiberwise loss and a missing-boundary loss.  More fundamentally, the
  optimal DQI interference is an exact directional sensitivity of the classical
  ML success probability; in the radial setting, the resulting area law links
  the entire DQI-deficit curve to the normalized log reliability.  Applied to
  an existential family of full-rank linear syndrome maps at each fixed rate,
  it gives an explicit Shannon threshold,
correct-decoding exponent, and limiting optimal DQI score.  A canonical
section simultaneously maximizes every radial layer coefficient.  The
selected ambiguous mass gives a quantitative no-gain bound, while explicit
families show a strict constant separation when that budget persists.  One
family is connected, coordinate-indecomposable, and linear-time; a
complementary family has positive rate and polynomial-time exact ML.  The
DE-NFG construction remains an offline realization of the pair functional,
not an automatic decoding gain.  Together, these results give an exact
optimizer, a reliability--interference identity, and matched sharpness and
stability regimes for objective-aware decoder co-design.

\section{The Uniform-Prior Endpoint}
\label{app:uniform}

At $p=1/2$ with uniform coordinate rewards $\gamma_i=1$, every transversal
has $2^\rho$ words and common acceptance $2^{\rho-m}$.  The DQI optimization
becomes an induced-edge problem in the hypercube.  Nonuniform rewards require
a separate weighted endpoint problem and are not covered by this proposition.

\begin{proposition}[Exact optimum at the uniform endpoint]
\label{prop:uniform}
For every binary linear code with redundancy $\rho$,
\begin{equation}
 P_D(1)\le\rho2^{\rho-1}.
 \label{eq:uniform-edge}
\end{equation}
Choose any $\rho$ linearly independent columns of $H$.  The coordinate
$\rho$-subcube supported on those columns is a transversal and attains
equality.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \max_D\mathcal J_D=\frac12+\frac{\rho}{2m}\qquad(p=1/2).
 \label{eq:uniform-score}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The edge-isoperimetric inequality for the $m$-cube states that a set $A$
induces at most $|A|\log_2|A|/2$ edges~\cite{harper1966}.  Substituting
$|A|=2^\rho$ gives~\eqref{eq:uniform-edge}.  If $I$ indexes $\rho$
independent columns, the restriction of $H$ to the coordinate subspace on $I$
is bijective onto $\F_2^\rho$.  That subcube is therefore a transversal and
attains the isoperimetric bound.  Equation~\eqref{eq:uniform-score} follows
from Theorem~\ref{thm:section-identity} by continuity at $q=1$.
\end{proof}

When the code dimension grows linearly, the common acceptance $2^{-(m-\rho)}$
is exponentially small.  Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform} is an exact
combinatorial endpoint, not an efficient postselection claim.

\section{Fixed-Instance Signs and a No-Free-Lunch Boundary}
\label{app:fixed-v}

Before averaging a known binary instance offset $v\in\F_2^m$, a syndrome-edge
term can carry a sign.  With a reference section $D^0$ and labels $c_s\in C$,
the sign on the edge $t=s+h_i$ is
\begin{equation}
 (-1)^{v\cdot(D^0(s)+e_i+D^0(t)+c_s+c_t)}.
 \label{eq:fixed-sign}
\end{equation}
The label-dependent terms are vertex gauges.  Around a closed syndrome walk,
the gauge-invariant product is $(-1)^{v\cdot c_\gamma}$, where
$c_\gamma\in C$ is the physical codeword traced by the walk.  All cycle signs
can be gauged positive exactly when $v\in C^\perp=\operatorname{row}H$.
Thus binary fixed-instance models require signed real factors; complex
characters become intrinsic only over larger alphabets.

There is also an algorithmic quantifier trap.  At the uniform point, consider
a linear section $D_G(s)=Gs$ with $HG=I_\rho$.  Its Walsh output is the basis
label
\begin{equation}
 x=G^Tv.
 \label{eq:right-inverse-output}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[An instance-aware right inverse can hard-code an answer]
\label{prop:no-free-lunch}
If $v\notin\operatorname{row}H$, then for every target $x\in\F_2^\rho$ there
exists a right inverse $G$ with $G^Tv=x$.  If $v=H^Ta$ lies in
$\operatorname{row}H$, every right inverse satisfies $G^Tv=a$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $v\notin\operatorname{row}H$, the augmented matrix
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H\\v^T\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ has rank $\rho+1$.
For each column $j$, solve
\begin{equation}
 \begin{bmatrix}H\\v^T\end{bmatrix}g_j
 =\begin{bmatrix}e_j\\x_j\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The resulting $G$ obeys both required equations.  If $v=H^Ta$, then
$G^Tv=G^TH^Ta=(HG)^Ta=a$.
\end{proof}

Choosing $G(v)$ to output an optimizer first requires knowing that optimizer;
the linear system then only encodes it in the decoder.  Meaningful
fixed-instance co-design therefore uses one uniform compact rule for all $v$
or charges the full cost of constructing the instance-dependent decoder.
